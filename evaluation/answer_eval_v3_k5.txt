  A: To share a Notion page with a guest, follow these steps:

1. Open the page you want to share and click **Share** at the top of the page.  
2. In the sharing dialog, enter the guest’s email address. If the address is **not** on any of your organization’s allowed domains, Notion will treat the person as a guest (otherwise they’ll be added as a regular member).

Q2 [direct] How can I change my Notion plan?

  A: You can change your Notion plan from the desktop or web app by opening the Settings menu in the sidebar.

If you want to request an upgrade or add‑on (and the workspace owner has enabled it), the owner can turn on the option under **Settings → Requests**. Non‑owners can then click **Request** next to the desired plan or add‑on.

**Mobile note**  
Plans bought through the desktop/web can only be changed there. If you subscribed via the iOS or Android app, manage the subscription in the Apple App Store or Google Play instead. On mobile you can tap ••• → **Upgrade plan** (or **Manage plan** if it’s an in‑app subscription) and follow the prompts.

Q5 [direct] How do I import a CSV file into Notion?
  expected: import-data.md | status: answered | score: 0.92 | corrected: None
  A: To bring a CSV file into Notion, follow these steps:

1. **Open the Import dialog**  
   - In Notion, go to **Settings → Import → CSV**, **or** type **/csv** on any page and select the CSV importer.

2. **Upload the file**  
   - Choose the **.csv** file you exported (for example, from Excel) on your computer and upload it.

3. **Pick where the data goes**  
   - Decide whether you want Notion to **create a new database** from the CSV or **add the rows to an existing database**.  
   - Select the page or location where the new database should appear.

4. **Map the columns**  
   - For each column in the CSV, match it to the appropriate Notion property (e.g., text, number, date, etc.). This step ensures the data lands in the right fields.

5. **Finish the import**  
   - Confirm the mapping and let Notion import the rows. Each CSV row becomes a Notion page/item, and each column becomes a property in the database.

*Alternative:* If you’re already inside a Notion database, you can also add a CSV directly from that database’s menu by selecting “Import CSV” and following the same upload and mapping steps.

Q8 [direct] How do database automations work?
  expected: database-automations.md | status: answered | score: 0.90 | corrected: None
  A: Database automations run whenever a specified change occurs in a Notion database. Each automation is built from two parts:

* **Triggers** – the events (such as a status change, a new page being added, etc.) that start the automation.  
* **Actions** – what happens after the trigger fires (for example, assigning a task, sending a Slack message, or running a button‑property command).

When you set up an automation, you choose whether it applies to every page in the whole database or only to pages that appear in a particular view (including any view filters). After you add one or more triggers and one or more actions, you save the automation and it will automatically execute each time the trigger condition is met. Guests with full access cannot create these automations.

Q9 [direct] How can I set a reminder in Notion?
  expected: reminders.md | status: answered | score: 0.90 | corrected: None
  A: To set a reminder in Notion, follow these steps:

1. **In a page**  
   - Type `@remind` and then specify the time, date, or both (e.g., `@remind tomorrow`, `@remind 7 pm`, or `@remind Wednesday at 1 pm`).  
   - A blue reminder tag will appear in the block.

2. **Adjust the reminder (optional)**  
   - Click the blue tag. A calendar window opens where you can fine‑tune the date, time, how early you want to be notified (e.g., 30 minutes before), the date format, and the timezone.

3. **For other people**  
   - Mention the person in the same block and add the reminder, e.g., `@Alyssa @remind next Monday 9 am to review this proposal`.

4. **In a database (if it has a Date property)**  
   - Use the Date property to set a reminder directly on that entry, following the same `@remind` syntax.

Once saved, Notion will send a notification at the scheduled time, and the reminder will turn red when it becomes “@Today” or is past due.

Q11 [direct] What keyboard shortcuts are available in Notion?
  expected: keyboard-shortcuts.md | status: answered | score: 0.89 | corrected: None
  A: Notion supports a full set of keyboard shortcuts (and Markdown shortcuts) so you can work without using the mouse. Key points from the documentation are:

* **General shortcuts** – Throughout the app you’ll see `cmd` (on Mac) or `ctrl` (on Windows/Linux) used in the shortcuts. They work with an English/QWERTY layout (other layouts are coming later).

* **Notion AI** – The default shortcut to open Notion AI is **Shift + Cmd/Ctrl + J**. You can change this key combo in **Settings → Preferences** under *Keyboard shortcut for Notion AI*.

* **Slash commands** – Press **/** to open the block‑insertion menu, then type the name of the block you want (e.g., `/heading`, `/quote`, `/table`, etc.). This lets you quickly add any type of content block.

* **Embeds** – You can embed content from more than 500 apps. While the docs only mention that there are “named embeds” and that you can use **/embed** for almost any service, the shortcut for invoking an embed is simply the slash command followed by “embed”.

* **Linking and page creation shortcuts** – Typing `[[` brings up a dropdown that prioritises linking to existing pages, whereas typing `+` brings up a dropdown that prioritises creating a new page.

These are the main keyboard shortcuts highlighted in the Notion help article. (The full list of all individual shortcuts isn’t reproduced here, but the article notes that Notion is “fully equipped with keyboard and Markdown shortcuts” covering essentially every action you might need.)

Q20 [reworded] Is there a way to get notified about a deadline in Notion?
  expected: reminders.md | status: answered | score: 0.57 | corrected: None
  A: Yes. In Notion you can set up reminders that will notify you (and others) when a deadline approaches.  

* **In a page** – type `@remind` followed by a date or time (e.g., `@remind next Monday 9 am`). The reminder appears as a blue tag; click it to adjust the exact time, how early you want the alert, the date format, and the timezone. These reminders and notifications ensure you (and any assigned collaborators) are alerted when a deadline is near or overdue.

Q26 [direct] What is the difference between the Can edit and Can comment permission levels in Notion?
  expected: sharing-and-permissions.md | status: answered | score: 0.56 | corrected: None
  A: **Can comment** – Users can only add comments to a page. They cannot change any of the page’s content or share the page with others.  

**Can edit** – Users can modify the page’s content (edit text, blocks, etc.), but they still cannot share the page with anyone else.  

So the key difference is that *Can comment* limits a user to commenting only, while *Can edit* gives a user the ability to edit the page’s content (but not to share it).

Q70 [direct] What is a restricted member, and how are they different from a regular member?
  expected: whos-who-in-a-workspace.md | status: answered | score: 0.90 | corrected: None
  A: A restricted member is a type of workspace user who has most of the same abilities as a regular member— they can log in with SSO, create private pages, use Notion AI (which respects whatever permissions they have), be placed in permission groups, and are billed the same way as a normal member.  

The key differences are:

* **Teamspace creation** – they cannot create new teamspaces.  
* **Sharing limits** – they can share pages only with people who are in the same teamspaces or pages as they are, and they can @‑mention only those members they share access with.  
* **Connect access** – the Connect option does not appear for them in Settings, so they cannot link other apps to Notion.  
* **Access level** – the teamspace member role treats restricted members the same as regular members, so a restricted member cannot be given a lower level of access than a regular member; to grant them less access you must use permission groups or individual permissions.  

In short, a restricted member is a billed workspace participant who can do almost everything a regular member can, except they cannot create teamspaces, have limited sharing/@‑mention capabilities, and do not have access to Connect.
